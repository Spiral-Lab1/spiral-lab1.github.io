About Our Lab

Welcome to the SPIRaL Lab at Emory Rollins School of Public Health. We use wearable sensors as tools to understand how light, temperature, and other environmental exposures influence chrono-health in real-world settings. Many aspects of human physiology and health (e.g., hormones, sleep, mood, performance) are cyclical and influenced by the body's internal timing system ("body clocks" or circadian rhythms). "Chrono-health" refers to the role of this endogenous timing system, and the external factors that influence it (e.g., light), in human and population health.

Current Research Areas

Light Epidemiology

Using data from body-worn sensors to investigate light as an environmental exposure in exposome and public health research.

Chrono-health in Epilepsy

Evaluating the relationship of environment, rhythms, and sleep to seizures, health, and quality of life in epilepsy.

Chrono-health in the General Population

Examining the roles of daily and seasonal rhythms to health from an epidemiological perspective in large, population-based studies. Areas of interest include light and other environmental exposures, behavior patterns, and occupational health (e.g., working at night).

Temperature and Heat Exposure

Collecting person-based measures of temperature to investigate health effects of heat.
